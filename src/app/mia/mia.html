Mia: helping modern dating feel a little less awkward.

From decoding messages to planning your next move, bridging the gap between matching and meeting.

Role

Sole UX/UI Designer

Team

Product Manager, Engineers

As the sole designer, I worked closely with product managers and engineers to translate product requirements into a clear, user-focused design direction for Mia.

I owned the end-to-end design process, from defining user flows and interaction patterns to designing the interface, prototyping AI conversations, testing edge cases, and preparing developer handoffs to ensure the experience translated smoothly from design to production.

1. Problem Statement

The interesting UX problem began
after matching.

Users already know each other's interests, lifestyle, personality and things in common. But having that information doesn't mean knowing how to use it in a conversation. "Both love live music" is useful. Knowing how to turn it into a natural message is far more useful.

The Problem

After a match, people freeze. They have shared context but no idea how to use it.

The Problem

Mia, a separate AI layer that suggests replies, openers and date ideas from shared interests.

The principle

Mia → User → Match
The person stays in control.

2. How we achieved this

Mia suggests. The user decides.

Instead of generating and sending messages, MIA is a separate conversational layer users enter on purpose. That gives them a psychologically safer place to ask what they might hesitate to ask their match.

3. Research

Competitive Analysis: Strengths & Gaps in existing solutions

I reviewed two major dating apps that provides AI assistance and uncovered key insights:

Hunch

Pros

Instant date planning:

Tagging eSia suggests venues and times immediately so matches move offline fast.

Cons

Feels rushed:

Strict timers and an aggressive push to meet leave little room for slow-paced texting.

Pros

High-intent ecosystem:

Curated daily drops and chat timers keep users focused on meeting in real life.

Cons

No banter support:

Sia handles logistics, offering zero help to socially anxious users on what to say.

Schmooze

Pros

Organic vibe matching:

Voice chats with Riya and meme-swiping match users on real humor and personality.

Cons

Chats stall online:

Makes starting a conversation easy, but does nothing to help plan an actual date.

Pros

Effortless icebreakers:

Built-in chat AI eliminates awkward "hey" openers with witty, low-pressure lines.

Cons

Scripted banter:

Over-reliance on AI-generated lines can feel robotic once you meet in person.

4. Key Opportunity

How Mia bridges the gaps

Reviewing Hunch and Schmooze revealed clear blind spots in AI dating support, shaping how we built Mia:

Authentic Prompts Over Scripted Lines:

Instead of feeding generic, copy-paste pickup lines that feel robotic, Mia tailors suggestions to real personality insights so replies sound genuinely like you.

Bridging Small Talk to Real Connection

While Schmooze leaves chats stalled in endless banter and Hunch rushes straight to venues, Mia guides conversations smoothly from breaking the ice to building real readiness to meet.

How to reply?
Explain their message

Help Beyond Just Date Planning:

Sia only handles date logistics—leaving daters stuck when they need real conversational help like decoding a match’s message, figuring out what to reply, or deciding their next move. Mia supports the entire conversation journey at your own pace.

5. the ux decisions that mattered

Three decisions that shaped Mia

1
AI should support authenticity, not replace it

Composing everything would be faster, but conversations would feel manufactured. MIA behaves as guidance, not an autonomous participant.

2
Context beats clever prompting

Users shouldn't explain their match to the AI every time. The product already holds the context, so the interface does that work.

3
AI needed its own space

"This conversation is with my match. This conversation is where I get help." That prevents mixing up suggestions with messages actually sent.

6. results

In early user testing, 4 out of 5 users said Mia made it easier to start conversations without overthinking

Rather than relying on generic pickup lines, testers used Mia’s context-aware prompts to break the ice and keep chats moving naturally. Most users treated Mia as a starting nudge, tweaking the suggested tone to match their own voice, which validated our core goal of assisting users without replacing their authenticity.
